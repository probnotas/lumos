The Number

June 11, 2026

Most companies start with a product. We started with a number.
852 million. That is how many people lie awake every night, or wake up too early, or sleep a full eight hours and still feel like they were hit by a truck. We kept coming back to that number because nobody seemed angry enough about it. The entire industry had decided that telling you your sleep was bad counted as solving it.
So we put everything on a table and started taking the problem apart.
The table is still there. Research papers covered in our handwriting. Circuit boards in pieces. A device we have redrawn more times than we can count. It does not look like a company yet. It looks like three people refusing to accept that the most important thing your body does has no real tool built for it.
That is what we are. Three people with AI backgrounds who decided sleep was worth more than the engineering jobs we walked away from. We won the TKS Toronto Moonshot Hub against 400 students and presented at Toronto Tech Week, but none of that mattered as much as the moment we realized the science to fix sleep already existed and nobody had bothered to put it into one device.
That device is Architect. A device that reads your brain in real time and intervenes at every stage of the night. It does not watch your sleep. It rebuilds it. Every intervention traces back to peer reviewed research from Tübingen, Northwestern, and the published neuroscience of sleep. We are not inventing biology. We are building the thing the research has been waiting for.
Right now we are leaving the part where you read papers and entering the part where you make something real. The table is shifting from theory to hardware. Soon it becomes something you can hold, wear, and feel working while you sleep.
Little is expected of three people building brain computer interfaces out of a small studio. That is the advantage. We are free to attempt the unreasonable thing. Long after we are gone, no one may remember our name. But someone, somewhere, may remember the first night they finally slept the way they were supposed to.
The papers are nearly read. The prototype is next.
Goodnight.
