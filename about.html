How we got here.

It started as a personal failure. We were the kids who slept a full eight hours and still woke up feeling hit by a bus. We set five alarms. We went to bed early. We tried every app on the store. Nothing worked, because the problem was never discipline. It was sleep itself, and somehow nobody had built a real fix for the most important thing your body does.

Here is the uncomfortable truth: sleep is the one thing you cannot fake. Your screens push back the melatonin that is supposed to knock you out. Your schedule drags your body clock around like a ragdoll. Stress keeps you wired when you should be winding down. That coffee from noon is still partying in your bloodstream at midnight. And the gadgets that promise to help? They just watch. A ring, a watch, an app, all politely informing you that you slept badly, as if that counts as solving it. 852 million people lose this fight every single night.

So we decided to build the thing that actually does something about it. We call it Architect: a device you wear that reads your brain while you sleep and steps in, live, at every stage of the night. It does not track your sleep. It rebuilds it. Every move it makes is backed by real, peer reviewed neuroscience out of Tübingen and Northwestern. We are not inventing biology. We are finally building the thing the research has been screaming for.

People started to notice. We won the TKS Toronto Moonshot Hub out of 400 students, presented at Toronto Tech Week, and made the Final Round of an Emergent Ventures grant. None of that is the point. But it kept telling us the same thing: we are not the only ones who think this needs to exist.

We are small on purpose. Three people, no managers, no growth targets, no meetings about meetings. We would rather build one device that changes a single night than a hundred apps that change a number on a chart.

Right now the science is done and the soldering is about to start. This summer we build Architect's first prototype at a hardware lab in Toronto. Theory is becoming something you can hold in your hands, strap on, and feel working while you are asleep.

This is the part where it stops being an idea. We are just getting started, and Architect is coming.
